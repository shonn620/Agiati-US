## 1. Executive Summary

AGIATI is a Korean pilates and activewear brand with a feminine, minimal, studio-to-street aesthetic. The U.S. launch positions AGIATI not as an imported K-fashion label, but as a **Korean wellness lifestyle brand** — one that brings the precision, restraint, and intentionality of Korean design philosophy to the American activewear market.

### The Opportunity

The U.S. activewear market reached $90B in 2025 and is projected to hit $97B in 2026. Within that market, a clear white space exists: **premium, feminine, pilates-first activewear with a Korean design sensibility**. Brands like Lululemon and Alo Yoga dominate the upper end but feel either too athletic, too trend-driven, or too American. A wave of consumers — particularly millennial and Gen Z women aged 22–38 — are gravitating toward a quieter, more refined aesthetic in their workout wardrobe: matte fabrics, neutral palettes, clean silhouettes, and a studio-to-street versatility that doesn't sacrifice style for function.

AGIATI sits perfectly in this opening.

### The Business Model Advantage

Sourcing product at ~25% of Korean retail price creates a significant margin opportunity. At U.S. premium price points (modeled below), the business can achieve **60–70% gross margins** — comparable to Alo Yoga and well above the activewear industry average. This margin structure supports paid acquisition, influencer seeding, and brand-building investment without sacrificing profitability.

### The Core Strategy

| Phase | Timeline | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation | Weeks 1–4 | Brand identity, content system, waitlist |
| Pre-Launch | Weeks 5–8 | Instagram/TikTok build, influencer seeding |
| Launch | Week 9–12 | Shopify store open, first drop, email/SMS |
| Growth | Month 4–6 | Repeat purchase, UGC, expand product mix |

### Key Positioning Statement

> *AGIATI is the Korean art of moving well. Minimal activewear designed for the woman who moves through her day with intention — from reformer to café, from studio to street.*

### 2.2 Inferred Brand Identity (to be confirmed with visuals)

Based on the dominant aesthetic of premium Korean pilates brands:

**Visual Aesthetic**
- Clean, editorial photography — often in neutral, minimal studio or outdoor settings
- Models in poses that suggest controlled, graceful movement rather than high-intensity sport
- Color palette likely built around nudes, creams, stone, slate, dusty rose, and black
- Little to no graphic branding visible on garments (logo-discreet or unbranded look)

**Product Design Language**
- Flattering cuts with pilates-specific design details: wide waistbands, cross-back bra straps, flared silhouettes

**Brand Personality**
- Feminine but not overtly "girly"
- Minimal but not cold or clinical
- Curated and thoughtful — the Korean design philosophy of *less, but better*

### 2.5 What Makes AGIATI Different from U.S. Brands

Most U.S. activewear brands fall into one of two buckets:

1. **Performance-led** (Lululemon, Nike, Under Armour) — technical fabrics, athletic positioning, gender-neutral or men-first design language
2. **Trend-led** (SKIMS, Set Active, Gymshark) — color drops, hype cycles, social-first, less focused on fit and function

AGIATI's differentiator is **design-led restraint**. Korean activewear brands approach workout clothing the way Japanese brands approach everyday basics — with attention to proportion, drape, and the relationship between garment and body. This is not widely available at accessible premium price points in the U.S.

Additionally, the Korean wellness narrative is a genuine cultural asset. K-beauty has broken $2B in U.S. sales and the "Korean wellness" category is expanding rapidly. AGIATI can ride this cultural momentum without being derivative — because it represents the next frontier: **K-wellness meets activewear**.

AGIATI should occupy the **upper-lifestyle quadrant** — premium but not pretentious, design-forward but functional, and always grounded in the studio practice rather than the runway.

### 3.3 Brand Pillars

**1. Korean Design Philosophy**
The Korean approach to design — *less, with more intention* — is the foundation. Clean lines, careful proportions, and fabrics that move with the body rather than against it.

**2. The Practice**
AGIATI is built around pilates: controlled, precise, graceful movement. This is not a brand for the 5AM CrossFit crowd. It's for the woman who arrives at the studio 10 minutes early, who chooses her outfit the night before, who sees movement as a form of self-care.

**3. Studio to Street**
Every piece should work as hard outside the studio as inside it. A coffee after class, a walk, a grocery run — AGIATI travels with you.

**4. Quiet Confidence**
No loud logos. No aggressive colorways. No performance claims you have to prove. Just garments that make you feel composed.

### 3.4 Naming & Localization Notes

The name **AGIATI** should be kept as-is. It is:
- Distinctive and memorable in the U.S. market
- Easy to pronounce (ah-gee-AH-tee)
- Carries a sense of Italian/European elegance while being of Korean origin — a rare combination that signals sophistication

Consider adding a pronunciation guide subtly in the website footer or About page. Do not over-explain the name; let it carry mystery.

### 5.2 Key Competitor Insights

**Korean competitors (XEXYMIX, Andar)** have broken into the U.S. market but have not established a premium brand identity. They compete on value, not positioning. AGIATI should not compete with them — it should exist in a different tier entirely.

## 6. Pricing Strategy

### 6.1 Pricing Framework

With sourcing at ~25% of Korean retail and a target of premium-but-accessible U.S. positioning, the pricing model looks like this:

**Assumption:** Korean retail price ≈ 2.5–3x the sourcing cost. U.S. pricing should reflect quality and positioning, not source geography.

|---|---|---|---|
| Bra Top / Sports Bra | $8–$14 | $58–$78 | ~82% |
| Crop Top / Tank | $9–$15 | $62–$82 | ~81% |

> Note: These are indicative estimates based on typical Korean activewear sourcing ratios. Adjust once actual sourcing invoices are confirmed.

### 6.2 Price Anchoring vs. Competitors

AGIATI should sit **between Alo Yoga and Set Active** in terms of sticker price, but position itself alongside Alo in terms of brand feel:

| Brand | Avg. Legging Price |
|---|---|
| Alo Yoga | $108 |
| Lululemon | $118 |
| **AGIATI** | **$92–$108** |
| Set Active | $72 |
| Vuori | $96 |

This creates a perception of **equal quality at a slightly better value** vs. Alo/Lululemon — a smart entry position that allows the brand to move prices upward as brand equity builds.

### 6.3 Launch Pricing

**Recommendation: 20% Launch Discount**

- A **15% discount** feels too small to generate urgency. It won't move people off the fence.
- A **20% discount** is meaningful, feels like a real reward for early adopters, and is easy to communicate ("You're getting 20% off as a founding customer").
- A **30% discount** risks anchoring the brand as a sale brand from day one. Alo Yoga almost never discounts — that scarcity is a brand asset. Starting with 30% off undermines the premium positioning you're building.

**Launch Discount Structure:**
- **Waitlist members:** 20% off for the first 48 hours after launch
- **General launch:** 15% off for the first 7 days (site-wide code)
- **Bundle discount:** 10% additional off when buying 2+ pieces (stacks with launch discount)
- **No further discounting** for at least 60 days post-launch

### 6.4 Bundle Pricing

| Bundle | Regular Price | Bundle Price | Savings |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Studio Set (bra top + leggings) | $166–$186 | $148–$168 | ~10% |
| The Full Look (bra + leggings + jacket) | $256–$296 | $228–$268 | ~11% |
| Two-Pair Legging Bundle | $176–$216 | $158–$194 | ~10% |
| Accessories Add-On (any order +$45) | — | Flat rate | — |

### 6.5 Long-Term Pricing Discipline

- **No Amazon.** Protect the brand channel.
- **No Nordstrom Rack / TJ Maxx.** Never let inventory end up in off-price retail.
- **Seasonal sale:** One annual "Archive Sale" (end of season) at 20–25% off select styles only
- **No permanent sale section.** This is brand suicide for a premium label.

## 7. Waitlist Strategy

### 7.1 Why a Waitlist Before Launch

A waitlist does three things a regular launch cannot:

1. **Validates demand** before investing in inventory and paid acquisition
2. **Builds a qualified audience** — people who sign up for a waitlist are 10–20x more likely to buy than cold traffic
3. **Creates psychological investment** — the wait makes the launch feel like an event worth attending

### 7.2 Waitlist Offer Recommendation

**Offer: 20% off + Free Shipping for early access (first 48 hours)**

Do not offer a discount alone — pair it with early access. The message is:
> You're not just getting a discount. You're getting to shop before anyone else.

This reframes the offer from "we're giving money away" to "you're part of the founding circle."

Do not ask for too much information at sign-up. **Email address only.** Optional: first name. Do not ask for size, location, or Instagram handle at this stage — friction kills conversion.

### 7.3 Coming Soon Landing Page Copy

**Headline (Option A — Aspirational):**
> Move Like Seoul.

**Headline (Option B — Direct):**
> Korean Pilates Wear. Arriving in the U.S. Soon.

**Headline (Option C — Identity-led):**
> For the woman who moves with intention.

**Recommended Sub-headline:**
> AGIATI brings the precision and restraint of Korean design to your practice. Minimal activewear designed for pilates, barre, and everything in between.

**CTA Button:**
> Join the Waitlist

**Below the form (trust signals):**

### 7.5 Waitlist Growth Tactics

- **Instagram bio link** → waitlist page from day one

- **Story countdown sticker** → post a countdown to launch on Instagram Stories once a date is set
- **Studio partnerships** → leave a QR code card at local pilates studios in target cities (NYC, LA, Austin, Miami)

### 8.5 Should You Start with Instagram Before the Website?

**Yes. Absolutely.**

Do not wait until the website is finished to begin building social presence. Here is why:

- Instagram and TikTok take time to build. Every week you wait is a week of potential organic discovery lost.
- You can grow an engaged audience of 1,000–5,000 people before your store opens — and those are your first buyers.
- A "coming soon" Shopify page + Instagram presence is a complete pre-launch stack. You do not need more than that to begin.

**Recommended order of operations:**
1. Instagram account + bio → Day 1
2. TikTok account → Day 2
3. Waitlist landing page → Day 3–5
4. First Instagram post → Day 5–7
5. Shopify store (full) → Week 6–8
6. Influencer seeding → Week 7–8
7. Launch → Week 9–10

### 9.3 TikTok Strategy

**Handle:** `@agiati.us`

TikTok is your fastest path to awareness. The algorithm is less dependent on existing followers than Instagram. One viral video can generate thousands of waitlist signups.

**Video Concepts for Pre-Launch:**

### 9.4 Influencer Strategy

**Tier Recommendation: Micro-Influencers Only (for launch)**

Do not seed macro-influencers (500K+) at launch. Here is why:
- Macro influencers charge $2,000–$20,000+ per post
- Their audiences are broad — not the precision-targeted pilates community you need
- UGC from macro influencers often feels inauthentic
- Micro-influencers (5K–80K) in the pilates/wellness space have 5–10x higher engagement and 3x higher conversion rates

**Ideal Influencer Profile:**
- 5,000 – 80,000 followers
- Pilates, barre, or wellness-focused content
- Engagement rate > 3%
- Aesthetic aligns with AGIATI: neutral, clean, minimal
- Based in target cities: NYC, LA, Miami, Austin, Chicago
- Genuinely practices pilates (check their content history)

**Target influencer count for launch:** 10–15

**Seeding Brief (what to send):**
- 2–3 pieces from the launch collection (their choice if possible — people post what they love)
- A short brand card: who AGIATI is, the Korean design story, launch date
- No script. No required hashtags. Just: *"Post if you love it. Tag us if you feel comfortable. Your authentic reaction is what we want."*
- Include a personal discount code (10–15% off) to share with their audience

**Finding influencers:**
- Search Instagram: #reformerpilates #pilatescommunity #pilateslook #pilateslover
- Look at who tags Alo Yoga, Set Active, Lululemon in pilates content
- Use free tools: Modash (free trial), Later's influencer search, or manual discovery

## 11. Sales Strategy

### 11.1 How to Generate First Sales

The first 50 orders matter more than almost anything else. They establish social proof, generate reviews, and create the first UGC. Here is how to get them:

**1. Waitlist Conversion (highest confidence)**
If you build a waitlist of 500+ before launch, expect 5–15% conversion = 25–75 first orders. Optimize this by:
- Sending the launch email at 9AM on a Tuesday or Wednesday (highest open rates)
- Making the code simple and easy to remember (e.g., `FOUNDING20`)
- Including a 48-hour countdown to create urgency

**2. Influencer-Driven Sales**
10–15 seeded micro-influencers posting on or near launch day = estimated 20–80 orders depending on influencer audience size and engagement. Each influencer gets a personal discount code to track their impact.

**3. Founder Network**
Do not underestimate the power of your personal network. Tell every person you know — colleagues, friends, family, classmates — that AGIATI is launching. Ask them to share. Offer founding customer pricing (same 20% off code). The first 10–20 orders often come from this circle.

**4. Local Studio Outreach**
Visit 3–5 pilates studios in your city (or nearest target market city). Introduce yourself to the owner or instructor. Leave cards, offer a studio discount code, ask if you can leave product for instructors to try. One studio with 100 active members is worth more than 1,000 cold Instagram followers.

**5. Social Launch Content**
On launch day, post across Instagram and TikTok. Create a "We're live" moment. Pin a launch video to your TikTok profile. Update your Instagram bio to "Now shipping to the U.S." This content alone can drive direct traffic.

### 11.2 Launch Offer Structure

| Audience | Offer | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Waitlist members | 20% off + free shipping | 48 hours |
| General public | 15% off with code `AGIATI15` | 7 days |
| Influencer audiences | 10–15% off with creator code | 30 days |
| Bundle buyers | Additional 10% off 2+ items | Ongoing |

### 11.3 Referral Strategy

After launch (Month 2), activate a referral program:

- **Tool:** Referral Candy or Gatsby (Shopify apps)
- **Offer:** Give $15, get $15 — customer refers a friend, both get $15 credit
- **Why this works for AGIATI:** Pilates communities are highly social. Women recommend their workout clothes to each other. This is word-of-mouth systematized.

### 11.4 Limited Drop Strategy (Month 2+)

Rather than keeping all styles permanently in stock, consider introducing a **limited drop model** for select colorways or styles:

- Announce the drop 5–7 days in advance via email and Instagram
- Open for 72 hours (or until sold out)
- Restock based on demand signals
- Creates urgency and a "collector" mentality without manufactured scarcity

This is how Set Active became a cult brand — drops create community ritual.

**Priority Month 1 is not revenue — it is data.** Learn which styles sell, which sizes run out, what your customers are asking, where they're coming from. Every piece of data in Month 1 makes Month 3 significantly more profitable.
